Global alignment

Needleman–Wunsch, 1970

Aligns the two sequences end to end: every residue is placed and gaps may fall anywhere, including the ends, where they are still penalised. Best when the sequences are homologous along their whole length and of similar size — two orthologous genes, two full proteins. Matrix values may go negative; the optimal path is traced from the bottom-right corner back to the top-left origin.

Local alignment

Smith–Waterman, 1981

Finds the single highest-scoring pair of subsequences and ignores the rest. Cell scores are never allowed below zero, so a poor stretch resets rather than dragging the score down. Best when a shared region sits inside otherwise dissimilar or unequal sequences — a conserved domain, a motif, a read against a genome. Traceback starts at the highest-scoring cell anywhere and stops as soon as the score returns to zero.

Both share the same core recurrence, and both can use affine gaps (Gotoh, 1982): opening a gap costs more than extending one, so a single long indel is favoured over many scattered ones. That two-parameter model is what the matrix below visualises.
